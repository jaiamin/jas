---
title: Design an experiment
description: Turn a question into a controlled, reviewable protocol.
---

## Choose the comparison

Start with a question that names the behavior, the change you want to test and
the outcome you will inspect. For example: does allowing messages change the
share contributed to a shared pool?

Use **Learn → Examples** for a reusable protocol, **Environments** to inspect
installed rules, or **New** in Experiments to begin in the editor.

## Define the protocol

**Quick** and **Advanced** edit the same protocol. Choose:

- **Environment:** the rules that determine observations, legal actions and
  consequences. A narrative supplies context; it does not replace those rules.
- **Agents:** participants, roles, models and memory settings.
- **Conditions:** the configurations being compared. Isolate the intended change.
- **Trials:** seeds, repetitions and horizon. Match seeds across conditions.
- **Outcomes and limits:** the headline measure, analysis plan and spending caps.

Review free checks and the estimated bounds before saving. Unknown prices remain
unknown and require a token ceiling before execution.

## Save, then run

**Save** freezes a protocol revision. A later edit creates another revision;
existing run evidence keeps its original protocol.

Saving does not start models. **Save and run**, or the experiment's run action,
explicitly authorizes execution within the configured limits.

## Work with the Assistant

Home starts a conversation from a question. The **Assistant** button in the global
header opens a side panel on other workspace pages; its four-corner control opens
the focused view.

On an individual experiment or run, the panel selects that record as context.
You can clear it or use **Add context** to choose another record. List pages do
not automatically select an experiment or run.

The Assistant can propose a protocol or analyze recorded evidence. Proposals are
pending until validated and reviewed. Sending a message can call your configured
model; opening the panel or choosing a suggestion does not.

## Interpret the comparison carefully

Models propose actions; environment rules settle them. Check participant-visible
information, missing or failed work, matched seeds and outcome definitions before
interpreting a difference. A synthetic comparison does not establish human behavior.
