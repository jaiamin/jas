Publishing shares a finished evidence record through a separate gallery. Local
experiments need no account; GitHub sign-in identifies the author when sharing.

## Browse and sign in

Open **Gallery** to browse public findings without an account. **My published**
contains GitHub sign-in and your publication controls. Sharing uses a separate,
model-free hosted service; your local workspace is never uploaded automatically.

Approve the device code in your browser. JAS updates automatically and stores the
publishing token in your operating system keychain. Linux requires a working
keychain service; JAS does not fall back to a plaintext token.

JAS uses the official gallery automatically when you open it. Browsing sends
ordinary request metadata to the service, never your local experiments or keys.
Local work does not depend on Gallery being available.

## Review before publishing

Open a finished experiment's **Publish** tab:

1. Describe the finding, choose a featured run and select public or unlisted visibility.
2. Review the exact public record. JAS scans it and checks recorded runs by replaying
   the rules without model calls.
3. Resolve scanner findings and inspect the evidence yourself.
4. Explicitly acknowledge the material and publish. A changed record needs review again.

The record includes the frozen protocol, observations, agent outputs, actions,
consequences, checkpoints, metrics and costs. Assistant conversations, provider
configuration and raw provider traces are excluded.

Participant-private observations become readable to anyone with the published
link. Review those observations as carefully as public messages. A scanner cannot
replace your review, and immutable evidence cannot be silently edited to hide a leak.

## Understand visibility and replay

**Unlisted** is the default. It is absent from discovery but readable by anyone
with the link. **Public** records can appear in Gallery. Each version is immutable.

The replay badge checks whether recorded actions reproduce recorded consequences
under trusted rules. It does not establish research validity or prove that a
claimed model produced the uploaded actions.

Find your records under **Gallery → My published**. Unpublishing stops the gallery
from serving the record and its files; already downloaded copies cannot be recalled.

## Fork a protocol

A public record offers a pinned fork command and a full download. Forking creates
a local experiment with source provenance. It neither calls a model nor installs
executable environment code. Review the protocol and choose your own models and
limits before explicitly running it.

See the [CLI reference](/reference/cli) for headless publishing and forking.
